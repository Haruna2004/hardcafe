A proposal for Yaba, Lagos · November 2026

A café where Lagos builds hardware.

Coffee at the front. Behind the glass: 24 kitted electronics seats, a wall of 3D printers, lasers and a CNC, a parts library, a drone cage and Lagos’s first shared wet lab. Run by a small team with AI tools.

The gap

Nigeria has builders without benches.

0shared wet labs open to founders in Lagos that our research could find
$7.5Mall African hardware & electronics equity raised in 2025 (Partech)
≈83kstudents at UNILAG and Yabatech, a short walk from a Yaba site

Demand is already visible: 116 teams entered the 2025/26 national robotics championship, Lagos parents pay ₦45k–₦100k a month for coding and robotics classes, and UNILAG paid out more than ₦250m for student prototypes in 2025. Drone makers are raising serious money and localising production. What’s missing is a place where anyone can walk in, pay for a day, and build.

The plan

Public at the front. Noise and chemicals at the back.

A ≈1,120 m² single-storey warehouse conversion. The café and parts shop face the street and pull people in. Members tap through a glass gate into the bench hall, and the print wall doubles as the window display. Workshops that are loud, dusty or chemical sit along a service “lab street” at the back, next to the loading door, extraction and power room.

Design notes

  • Wet lab gets its own door, sink, eyewash and shower, ducted hood. No food past the gate.
  • Lasers and CNC are ducted out of the back wall; floor tape marks the safe zones.
  • Roof: ≈30–50 kWp solar, LFP battery, standby generator only for long outages.
  • Classroom has its own street door, so kids never walk through the workshops.
  • Prayer and quiet room, restrooms and lockers off the café.

Operations

Two technical leads. Six non-technical staff. AI in every pocket.

Makerspaces fail on maintenance and staffing. Fab Foundation guidance implies 4–5 technical staff to run two shifts. We get there with fewer specialists by putting the know-how in software.

  • Parts runnerRequests from members’ phones go to the nearest staff member with the exact aisle and bin.
  • Machine watchPrinter cameras and sensors flag faults before they waste a day, with a step-by-step fix guide.
  • RestockStock drops below a minimum and a purchase order is drafted for a manager to approve.
  • Safety gatesMachines unlock only for members who passed the induction for that machine.
  • Build-your-own toolsManagers describe a problem and get a small internal app the same day.

Location

Yaba first.

UNILAG and Yabatech sit on one district, with three CafeOne hubs, CcHUB and a Red Line rail stop nearby. Rents run about ₦50k–₦127k per m² a year, far below the island. If no Yaba building works, Ebute-Metta next door has warehouse space on the same rail line.

Risks we’ve planned for

What others learned the hard way.

Single funder

Nairobi’s Gearbox closed in 2023 when its main donor (≈90% of funding) left; only its PCB assembly business survived. We run ten revenue streams and sell services from day one.

Capex-heavy sites

TechShop spent $2.5–3.5M per site and went into liquidation in 2017. We start with one lean site, partner space and leased solar.

Upfront rent

Lagos landlords often ask for two years upfront; the law caps it at one in most areas. Partner space or a one-year lease keeps cash for machines.

Maintenance

Budgeted at ≈12% of equipment value a year, plus AI fault detection, spare parts on the shelf and vendor service contracts.

Diesel and FX

Diesel moved between ₦940 and ₦2,024 a litre in 12 months. Solar and battery come first; equipment is bought in one batch at launch.

Safety

Machine inductions, BSL-1 scope only, LiPo safe storage, ducted extraction. Any GMO work would need biosafety agency approval and is out of scope.

Partnership models

Built with partners, not around one.

A bank

Co-brand the space with an existing café network, finance the solar system, and meet founders when they first need an account and a loan.

A university

Timetabled practical sessions for students, instead of building labs at every study centre.

Hardware companies

Sponsored seats and bootcamps that train the technicians and suppliers they need to hire.

The state

Equipment grants and shared-lab funding for a facility any Lagos founder can use.
