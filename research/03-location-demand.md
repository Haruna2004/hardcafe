# 03 · Location, demand and policy for a Lagos hardware coworking lab

Prepared 2026-11-17. Scope: where the flagship should go, what evidence of demand exists, and the policy and regulatory picture.

**How to read this note**

- `[S#]` links point to the numbered Sources list at the end. Each source carries its publication date or the date it was accessed.
- "(snippet)" marks a fact seen only in a search-result snippet, because the page blocked direct fetching. Treat these as lower confidence.
- **Estimate** marks my own arithmetic, and the working is shown each time.
- Naira-to-dollar conversions use **₦1,475/US$**. That is the midpoint of the ₦1,450–1,500 band Knight Frank reports for H2 2025 [S16]. I did not check the November 2026 rate.
- Research limits: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Some items are therefore flagged as unverified rather than guessed: NCDC/MLSCN rules for pathogen (non-GMO) BSL-2 work, Lagos State Safety Commission and LASEPA requirements, neighbourhood-level flood and crime data, and medtech/biotech equipment-gap evidence. The NCAA and Lagos Safety Commission websites could not be reached from this session.

## Key takeaways

1. **Put the flagship in Yaba.**
   - Yaba scores 4.40/5 on the weighted scorecard. The other five areas score 3.55 (Ebute-Metta/Oyingbo), 3.15 (Ikeja), 3.10 (Surulere), 2.45 (Lekki) and 2.10 (Victoria Island).
   - Yaba stays first under an alternative, customer-weighted scheme (section 1.2).
   - If Yaba floor plates are too small or too expensive, Ebute-Metta/Oyingbo, on the same rail corridor, is the fallback.
2. **Talent density.**
   - UNILAG, whose main campus is in Akoka, Yaba, had 52,106 students university-wide in 2024/25 [S32]. Yaba College of Technology had 30,671 students in March 2025 [S37]. Together that is about 82,800 students centred on one district (Estimate: 52,106 + 30,671; UNILAG's total includes its Idi-Araba medical campus).
   - UNILAG's engineering faculty graduated 613 students in January 2026, 89 of them with first-class degrees [S35].
   - Four Lagos public institutions admitted about 44,500 new students for 2025/26 (Estimate: UNILAG 9,179 + Yabatech 17,130 + LASU 14,893 + LASUSTECH 3,272) [S33][S38][S40][S42].
3. **Rail exists but is thin.**
   - Red Line Phase 1 runs 27 km over 8 stations, including Yaba, Oyingbo and Ikeja. Paid service began on 15 Oct 2024 [S1].
   - It runs only about 5–9 trips a day and carries about 100,000 trips a month. The Blue Line carries about 500,000 a month [S1][S3].
   - Three new 8-coach trains arrived in November 2026 but are not yet in service [S3].
   - None of the operating Red or Blue Line stations is in Lekki or Victoria Island [S1][S4].
4. **Island rents rule out a flagship there.**
   - Lagos prime offices average about $55/sqm/month [S16]. At that rate a 500 sqm floor costs about $330k a year, more than the entire $300k budget (Estimate: 55 × 12 × 500).
   - Yaba asking rents are ₦70,000/sqm/yr plus ₦40,000 service charge (snippet) [S20]. That is about $75/sqm/yr, or about ₦55m (about $37k) a year for 500 sqm (Estimate).
   - Ikeja's industrial axis averages about $3/sqm/month [S16].
5. **Sterling's Café One footprint already clusters in Yaba.**
   - cafeone.ng lists 37 hubs, 22 of them in Lagos [S22].
   - Lagos hubs include 3 in Yaba/Akoka (Herbert Macauley, Yaba-Ozone, Unilag), 4 around Ikeja, 4 on the Lekki–Ajah axis and 1 in Surulere. None is listed in Ebute-Metta/Oyingbo.
6. **Risk screens.**
   - Flood: NEMA's May 2026 list flags 15 Lagos LGAs as moderate flood risk. They include Lagos Mainland (Yaba, Ebute-Metta), Ikeja, Eti-Osa (VI, Lekki, Ikoyi [S105]) and Ibeju-Lekki. Surulere is not on the list [S11][S12].
   - Crime: Lagos's crime-records system ranked Ikeja, Lagos Island and Lekki as the top three crime locations for January 2024 to May 2025 [S14]. Lekki/Ajah also accounted for more than 16% of 35,461 court filings over three years [S15].
7. **Hardware capital is flowing, but to very few companies.**
   - Terra raised about $52M in seed rounds in 2026 ($11.75M in Jan, $22M in Feb, $18M in Aug) and runs a 15,000 sq ft factory in Abuja [S48][S50].
   - Koolboks raised an $11M Series A (Sep 2025) [S57], Arnergy an $18M Series B (Apr 2025) [S58] and Releaf $10M (Mar 2026) [S59].
   - Across Africa, Partech counted only $7.5M of "hardware and electronics" equity in 2025, down 72% over 5 deals. Cleantech equity, by contrast, rose 186% to $550M [S63].
   - Early hardware teams are starved of capital, and shared tools address exactly that gap.
8. **Defence localisation is pulling demand toward local suppliers.**
   - The DICON Act 2023 and the Defence Ministry's import restrictions (Aug 2024) push procurement toward Nigerian producers [S89].
   - DICON signed MoUs with Terra (Feb 2026) and with EIB Group/Briech UAS (Apr 2026) to localise drone production and build local supplier networks [S51][S55].
   - Miva Open University and Terra are setting up robotics, drone and VR labs, starting with an Abuja pilot (Aug 2026) [S52].
9. **Families pay for STEM, and schools compete in robotics.**
   - The 2025/26 FIRST national championship drew 116 teams and was won by Queen's College Lagos, a Yaba school [S71][S26].
   - WRO Nigeria 2025 drew 313 students from 44 schools in Lagos [S73].
   - Lagos prices: ₦45,000 for a 10-class virtual group coding course from a Yaba-based provider [S66], ₦50,000–₦100,000 a month for one-to-one coding/robotics [S67], and ₦65,000 a week for a camp with AI and robotics [S68].
10. **Policy support is real but slow.**
    - The Startup Act (2022) is still at "pre-implementation stage". Its 31+ incentives depend on 15+ government bodies (Aug 2026) [S78].
    - Under Nigeria First (May 2025), federal ministries and agencies may not buy foreign goods where local alternatives exist [S86].
    - The BPP–NASENI agreement explicitly names "surveillance drones" and "solar backup systems" as local products to prioritise [S87].
11. **Regulation is manageable with design choices.**
    - Drones: each needs an ONSA End-User Certificate and NCAA registration (open only to Nigerian owners and entities), and operators go through a five-phase certification [S90][S91][S93].
    - GMO wet-lab work: needs NBMA accreditation (₦500k processing + ₦100k permit), facility certification (₦500k + ₦50k) and an Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC) [S97][S99].
    - Chemicals: controlled imports need an ONSA certificate backed by a NAFDAC permit [S94].
12. **Co-funding exists.**
    - LASRIC: the state approved ₦1bn in 2024 and awarded about ₦75m to 15 grantees in August 2025. A Sterling Bank executive was on its council as named at the June 2024 relaunch [S79][S80].
    - Lagos Innovates workspace vouchers cover 30–75% of members' fees at 50+ partner hubs [S81].
    - 3MTT's ₦4bn outcome-based fund for training partners closes on 30 Nov 2026 [S47].
    - iDICE plans 66 hubs and has a $170M fund of funds [S84].
    - UNILAG awarded ₦250m+ for prototype development in 2025 [S36].

## 1. Location

### 1.1 Area evidence

**Yaba** (Lagos Mainland; Yaba LCDA)

- **Campuses and research institutes**
  - UNILAG's main campus is at Akoka, Yaba (802 acres). The university as a whole had 52,106 students in 2024/25, of whom 35,177 were undergraduates and 10,943 postgraduates [S32].
  - Yabatech had 30,671 students in March 2025 [S37].
  - Also in Yaba: the Federal College of Education (Technical) Akoka, Queen's College, the Federal Science and Technical College and the Nigerian Institute of Medical Research (NIMR) [S26].
  - Wikipedia lists NIMR with 2,000+ members of staff and two ISO 15189-accredited labs [S43].
- **Transit**
  - The Red Line's Yaba station is operating and connects to Yaba Bus Terminal [S1].
  - The terminal opened in June 2021, a few metres from the rail station site (snippet) [S9].
- **Tech ecosystem**
  - Wikipedia calls Yaba "the technological hub of Lagos". It is home to CcHUB (founded in 2011), Andela, BudgIT, Hotels.ng and Konga [S26].
  - CcHUB is expanding its Yaba headquarters with its first private startup offices (June 2026; snippet) [S25].
  - Yaba has 10 managed-office "units", 12.5% of the Lagos supply [S18].
- **Café One**
  - Three hubs: Herbert Macauley (Yaba), Yaba (Ozone) and Unilag (Akoka) [S22].
  - The E-Centre on Commercial Avenue houses Ozone Cinemas [S26]. Café One's mainland workspace opened there in 2022 (snippet) [S24].
- **Existing fabrication**
  - UNILAG's Fab Lab sits in the D.K. Olukoya Central Research Laboratory. It lists 3D printing, CNC milling and PCB production, and "engages 5–10 students daily" [S75].
  - That is small, and it suggests unmet capacity.
- **Rent signals**
  - Asking office rents are ₦50,000–₦70,000/sqm/yr, with one listing adding ₦40,000/sqm service charge (snippet) [S20].
  - Estate Intel found naira office rents in Yaba trending upward in H1 2025 [S17].
  - Knight Frank's 3-bed residential rent is ₦6.5m/yr in Yaba versus ₦23.3m in VI, as read from the report's chart data [S16]. This is a proxy for area price level (Estimate: 3.6× cheaper).
- **Flood risk:** the Lagos Mainland LGA is on NEMA's 2026 moderate-risk list [S11]. UNILAG's campus is on the Lagos Lagoon [S32], so check low-lying plots.
- **Crime:** "Lagos Mainland/Yaba" is among the "other high-case areas" for court filings, but not in the top three [S15][S14].

**Ebute-Metta / Oyingbo** (Lagos Mainland LGA)

- **Transit**
  - Oyingbo is the operating Red Line Phase 1 terminus and has a bus terminal [S1].
  - The Ebute Metta Red Line station is under construction, on the roughly 10 km Phase II to Marina. It will interchange with Mobolaji Johnson station, which serves Lagos–Ibadan trains [S1].
- **Area character:** the Nigerian Railway Corporation's headquarters and Oyingbo Market are here [S27].
- **Rent signals:** a 148 sqm warehouse on Simpson Street, off Herbert Macaulay Way, was advertised in June 2026 at ₦10m/yr, with a 10–15-year lease preferred (snippet) [S21]. That is about ₦67,300/sqm/yr, or about $46 (Estimate).
- **Ecosystem:** no Café One hub is listed here [S22], and the area is not in the top-10 managed-office areas [S18].
- **Flood risk and crime:** same LGA as Yaba [S11][S15].

**Ikeja**

- **Transit:** the Red Line's Ikeja station connects to Ikeja Bus Terminal [S1].
- **Campus:** LASU's College of Medicine campus is in Ikeja [S41].
- **Computer Village**
  - Described as "one of West Africa's largest ICT markets" [S28].
  - Lagos gave traders an 18-month window to relocate (reported Oct 2025) [S29]. But the Katangowa site was still at foundation level, and a June 2026 court ruling in the developer's favour sent the contract dispute to mediation [S28].
  - So the market's medium-term location is uncertain.
  - I could not verify whether Computer Village stocks embedded and electronic components, as opposed to phones and PCs.
- **Market signals**
  - Knight Frank reports a ~9,000 sqm technology retail mall under construction in Ikeja [S16]. Sidel and Beiersdorf moved into Ikeja's The Phoenix office development [S16].
  - Ikeja GRA had 3.88% office vacancy in H1 2025, and its naira rents are rising [S17].
  - The Ikeja industrial axis averages about $3/sqm/month [S16]. The 3-bed residential rent is ₦16m [S16].
- **Ecosystem**
  - 12 managed-office units, or 15% of the Lagos supply [S18].
  - Four Café One hubs: Ikeja, Aromire, Adebola House (Opebi) and Oregun [S22].
  - Vibranium Valley (42 Local Airport Rd, Ikeja) hosted a hardware/IoT prepaid-meter hackathon [S30].
- **Flood risk:** the Ikeja LGA is on the 2026 list [S11].
- **Crime:** Ikeja was ranked first among the top three crime locations (Jan 2024 to May 2025) [S14].

**Surulere**

- **Transit**
  - The Blue Line serves the National Theatre in Iganmu, Surulere [S4][S104].
  - Lagos plans to extend the Blue Line to the National Stadium [S7], which is in Surulere [S103].
- **Campus:** UNILAG's College of Medicine is at Idi-Araba, Surulere [S32]. That is relevant for wet-lab users.
- **Ecosystem:** 4 managed-office units (5%) [S18] and one Café One (Adeniran Ogunsanya) [S22].
- **Rent proxy:** 3-bed ₦7.1m and 4-bed ₦10.5m residential rents [S16]. Knight Frank expects Yaba and Surulere to see the fastest absorption of small units [S16].
- **Flood risk:** Surulere is not among the 15 Lagos LGAs on NEMA's 2026 list [S11][S12].
- **Crime:** not named in the state crime summaries [S14][S15]. That means no data, not "safe".

**Lekki**

- **Transit**
  - There is no operating rail station [S1][S4].
  - A regulated Lekki–Epe bus scheme started on 8 Dec 2025 with 229 buses [S8].
- **Ecosystem**
  - Lekki Phase 1 is the largest managed-office cluster, with 15 units (18.75%) [S18].
  - Four Café One hubs: Lekki, Chevron, Ikate and Ajah [S22].
  - Sterling unveiled Café One in Lekki in 2019 (snippet) [S23].
- **Talent:** LASU's Faculty of Engineering is on its Epe campus, not in Lekki [S41]. I did not verify other campuses on the corridor.
- **Rent proxy:** 3-bed/4-bed residential rents of ₦18m/₦28m in Lekki Phase 1 and ₦13m/₦16m in Ikate [S16].
- **Flood risk:** both Eti-Osa and Ibeju-Lekki are on the 2026 list [S11].
- **Crime:** Lekki is in the top three crime locations [S14], and Lekki/Ajah accounts for more than 16% of court filings [S15].

**Victoria Island**

- **Transit:** no operating rail station [S1][S4].
- **Ecosystem**
  - 13 managed-office units (16.25%) [S18], two Café One entries (Adeola Odeku and an "Event Store") [S22], and 9.84% office vacancy in H1 2025 [S17].
- **Rent:** prime offices average about $55/sqm/month [S16]. The 3-bed/4-bed residential rent is ₦23.3m/₦36.6m [S16].
- **Talent:** this research found no large engineering campus in VI.
- **Flood risk:** Eti-Osa is on the 2026 list [S11].
- **Crime:** neighbouring Lagos Island is in the top three [S14].

**Citywide context**

- **Traffic:** Numbeo's 2025 mid-year index ranked Lagos the world's most congested city, with a 70-minute average one-way commute [S10]. That makes walking and rail access to the site worth a lot.
- **Rail outlook:**
  - Lagos projects 20 Red Line trips a day and up to 500,000 passengers a day once Phase II to Marina is complete [S2]. These are projections, not current service.
  - Blue Line Phase II (Mile 2 to Okokomaiko, 14 km) includes a LASU station [S5]. FESTAC, Alakija and Trade Fair stations are due by end-February 2027 [S6], and the whole of Phase II by Q1 2027 [S7].
- **Flood context:** NIHSA's 2025 Annual Flood Outlook named Lagos among high-risk states (snippet) [S13].

### 1.2 Location scorecard

Scores run from 1 to 5, where 5 is the best of the six areas on that criterion. They are my judgements from the evidence in 1.1, not measured values.

The criteria and base weights are:

- **Talent** (25%): nearby engineering and science students and researchers.
- **Transit** (15%): rail and bus access.
- **Ecosystem** (15%): hubs, startups and managed offices.
- **Cost** (15%): rent level for workshop-capable space.
- **Flood** (10%): flood exposure.
- **Safety** (5%).
- **Fit** (15%): Café One/Sterling, CcHUB and university partners, plus access to paying users.

Safety is weighted low because the only data is LGA- or court-level.

| Area | Talent | Transit | Ecosystem | Cost | Flood | Safety | Fit | **Base score** | Alt score* | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yaba** | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 | **4.40** | 4.40 | 1 |
| **Ebute-Metta / Oyingbo** | 4 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | **3.55** | 3.25 | 2 |
| **Ikeja** | 2 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | **3.15** | 3.30 | 3 |
| **Surulere** | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4† | 4 | 3 | 3 | **3.10** | 3.00 | 4 |
| **Lekki** | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | **2.45** | 2.65 | 5 |
| **Victoria Island** | 1 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 | **2.10** | 2.50 | 6 |

**How the base score is built:** Σ(score × weight) / 100. For Yaba: (5×25 + 4×15 + 5×15 + 4×15 + 3×10 + 3×5 + 5×15) / 100 = 440 / 100 = **4.40**.

\*The alternative, customer-first weights are Talent 15, Transit 10, Ecosystem 20, Cost 10, Flood 10, Safety 10, Fit 25. Under them Yaba stays first, while Ikeja (3.30) edges past Ebute-Metta (3.25).

†No Surulere office rent was found. The cost score uses Knight Frank's residential proxy only [S16].

### 1.3 What a 500 sqm floor costs (Estimate)

The 500 sqm footprint is only an illustrative size for a makerspace, café, wet lab and drone cage. The design workstream should set the real number.

| Option | Basis | ₦/sqm/yr | US$/sqm/yr | 500 sqm per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yaba office (asking) | ₦70,000 rent + ₦40,000 service charge (snippet) [S20] | 110,000 | ~75 | ₦55.0m ≈ $37k |
| Yaba office, rent only | ₦70,000 [S20] | 70,000 | ~47 | ₦35.0m ≈ $24k |
| Ebute-Metta warehouse (asking) | ₦10m for 148.48 sqm (snippet) [S21] | ~67,300 | ~46 | ₦33.7m ≈ $23k |
| Ikeja industrial axis (average) | ~$3.0/sqm/month [S16] | ~53,100 | 36 | ≈ $18k |
| Prime free-zone industrial | $4.0–6.5/sqm/month [S16] | ~70,800–115,000 | 48–78 | ≈ $24k–39k |
| Lagos prime office (Grade A) | ~$55/sqm/month [S16] | ~973,500 | 660 | ≈ $330k |

**Break-even check (Estimate):** ₦55m a year is ₦4.58m a month. Lagos monthly workstations cost ₦150,000–₦170,000 [S19]. So about 29 desk-equivalents at ₦160,000 would cover the Yaba rent, including service charge (₦4.58m ÷ ₦160,000).

**Caveat:** the Yaba and Ebute-Metta figures are single asking prices, not transacted rents. Get agent comps before committing.

### 1.4 Recommendation (ranked)

1. **Yaba: flagship.**
   - It has the highest density of engineering and science students and researchers: UNILAG, Yabatech, FCE (Technical), NIMR and UNILAG's small Fab Lab [S26][S32][S37][S43][S75].
   - It is Sterling's densest student-area Café One cluster, with 3 hubs [S22], and CcHUB is expanding next door [S25].
   - Rail reaches it via the Yaba Red Line station [S1].
   - Mainland rents are a fraction of island rents [S16][S20].
   - **Site brief.** Look for ground-floor or light-industrial space:
     - within walking distance of Yaba station or bus terminal, or near the Café One Herbert Macauley and Yaba (Ozone) hubs [S22];
     - with three-phase power, extraction for the laser cutter and wet lab, and clear height for an indoor netted drone cage;
     - with a plot-level flood history from LASEMA.
2. **Ebute-Metta / Oyingbo: value alternative, or the workshop "back of house".**
   - It is the best rail node: the Oyingbo terminus today, plus a planned Ebute Metta Red Line station that interchanges with Lagos–Ibadan trains [S1].
   - Warehouse-grade stock is advertised at Yaba-office prices (snippet) [S21].
   - The ecosystem is weak, with no Café One and no managed-office cluster [S18][S22].
   - A split model could work: a Yaba café and coworking front end, with heavy tools in Ebute-Metta.
3. **Ikeja: later satellite, focused on the components store and drone/defence customers.**
   - Positives: Computer Village, the Ikeja Red Line station, four Café One hubs, and tight office demand [S1][S17][S22][S28].
   - Negatives: weak engineering-student density, the top crime ranking [S14], and an unresolved Computer Village relocation [S28][S29].
4. **Surulere: wet-lab satellite option.** It sits next to UNILAG's College of Medicine (Idi-Araba) [S32], is the only candidate not flagged on NEMA's 2026 list [S11], and has Blue Line access [S4][S7]. Its ecosystem is thin.
5. **Lekki: kids-STEM satellite, run through existing Café One Lekki, Chevron and Ikate hubs.** It is not suitable for a flagship: no rail, flood and crime flags, and island rents [S1][S11][S14][S16][S22].
6. **Victoria Island: investor-facing showroom at most.** Prime rents alone ($55/sqm/month) would exceed the budget [S16].

### 2.3 Hardware and deep-tech funding trends (Africa)

- **Partech 2025 report (Jan 2026):**
  - Total funding was $4.1B (+25%): $2.4B equity and a record $1.6B debt [S62].
  - Cleantech equity was $550M (+186%) and healthtech $215M (+232%) [S62][S63].
  - "Hardware and Electronics" equity was only $7.5M (−72%) over 5 deals [S63].
  - Nigeria raised $572M (−3%), of which $412M was equity (−21%) and $160M debt (+132%), over 102 deals. Cleantech was only 7% of Nigerian equity [S63].
- **Africa: The Big Deal (2025 to September):** $2.2B raised, 42% of it debt. Energy took 33% of funding, and climate-focused startups 39% [S64].
- **Briter (Jul 2026):** climate tech took 40% of African venture funding in 2025, about $1.5B [S65].
- **What this means:**
  - Hardware money in Africa is concentrated in late-stage energy and climate companies, often as debt, and in a handful of defence names.
  - Seed-stage hardware equity is close to zero.

## 3. Policy and regulation

### 3.1 Federal innovation and skills policy

- **Nigeria Startup Act 2022**
  - Signed 19 Oct 2022. As of August 2026 it "remains at the pre-implementation stage" [S78].
  - It has 31+ incentives in six areas: tax and fiscal, regulatory, funding, exports, ecosystem and training. Delivering them needs 15+ ministries, departments and agencies [S78].
  - 12,948 startups had registered on the labelling portal by April 2024 [S78].
  - Implication: treat Startup Act incentives as upside, not as part of the base case.
- **3MTT**
  - 179,000 fellows trained so far [S47].
  - Cohort 5 has about ₦4bn for "delivery partners", paid against milestones (learning, verified skills, job, six-month retention).
  - Partners must be registered in Nigeria and able to train 100+ fellows in six months.
  - Applications close 30 Nov 2026, and training starts January 2027 [S47].
  - **Not verified:** whether hardware or IoT tracks qualify.
- **NITDA**
  - It has funded a fab lab before: FabLab 1.0 at NCAIR, Abuja (2022) [S77].
  - It planned the national robotics curriculum from September 2025 [S73].
- **Tax:** the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 was signed on 26 Jun 2025 and took effect on 1 Jan 2026 [S16]. I did not verify specific incentives for labs or startups.

### 3.2 Lagos State bodies

- **LASRIC (Lagos State Science, Research and Innovation Council)**
  - The state approved ₦1bn at its June 2024 relaunch, to fund startup seed money, innovation research and school STEM programmes.
  - At the relaunch, its 13-member council included Sterling Bank's Sulaimon Abubakar plus LSETF and Eko Innovation Centre representatives. Applicants are assessed on the "POEM" framework (Proposition, Organisation, Economics, Milestones) [S79].
  - In August 2025 it gave about ₦75m to 15 awardees: 5 each in R&D, Innovation and STEM Education [S80].
- **LSETF / Lagos Innovates**
  - Workspace vouchers cover 30–75% of membership costs at 50+ partner hubs, for 3–12 months. Eligible startups must be registered with the Lagos State Inland Revenue Service [S81].
  - A round opened in January 2026 [S82].
  - The programme has supported 1,200+ startups and trained 3,000+ people since 2018 [S83].
  - Opportunity: become an accredited partner hub so that members can pay with vouchers.

### 3.3 Buy-Nigerian policy (2025–2026)

- **Nigeria First (May 2025)**
  - The Federal Executive Council directed the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP) to favour local suppliers and keep a register of qualified Nigerian manufacturers.
  - Ministries, departments and agencies are barred from buying foreign goods and services where local alternatives exist [S86].
  - An executive order was to give it legal backing (snippet) [S88]. I could not confirm that it was signed.
  - In May 2026 the government threatened sanctions on agencies that ignore it for vehicle purchases (headline) [S102].
- **BPP–NASENI MoU (Jul 2025)**
  - Prioritises NASENI products "from tractors to tablets, from surveillance drones to solar backup systems" and lists them on the procurement portal [S87].
  - Implication: locally built hardware gains a federal buyer, and a lab can sell prototyping and small-batch services to firms chasing that.

### 3.4 Defence local production

- **Legal base:** DICON Act 2023, endorsed November 2023 [S89].
- **Defence Ministry restrictions (Aug 2024)**
  - Agencies need Defence Ministry clearance to buy arms and ammunition.
  - No-objection certificates are barred for items made in Nigeria.
  - 7.62mm ammunition imports are to be phased out within two years [S89].
- **Recent deals**
  - The Armed Forces launched a Briech UAS-built attack drone (April 2025) [S54].
  - DICON–Terra MoU (Feb 2026): a joint-venture subsidiary of DICON for drone, robotics and cyber assembly, R&D and training. Capital and timelines were not disclosed [S51].
  - DICON–EIB Group (Briech UAS, Poctova) MoU (Apr 2026): end-to-end UAS production, a local supplier and SME network, and a planned "Defence Silicon Valley" hub [S55].
- **Implication:** there is real pull for local suppliers and engineers. The lab should keep a written civil-use and dual-use policy, for example no weapons integration on site and members' export-control and EUC compliance, so that bank and university partners are comfortable.

### 3.5 Drones: NCAA and ONSA rules, and what they mean for the test cage and store

- **Nigeria Civil Aviation Regulations 2023, Part 21 (as summarised by Templars, Jul 2025)**
  - Drones are classed by weight as nano, micro, small, medium or large, and by risk as open, specific or certified.
  - Every drone must be registered. Registration is limited to Nigerian citizens, permanent residents, Nigerian entities and government bodies.
  - Operators need a 5-year renewable RPAS Operator Certificate, security clearance, an end-user certificate and insurance [S90].
- **NCAA advisory (Mar 2026)**
  - Operators must get ONSA clearance and an End-User Certificate for each drone, register on the NCAA portal, and complete a five-phase certification.
  - Penalties include fines, imprisonment and seizure [S91].
  - The portal launched at DroneTecX in May 2026 [S31].
- **ONSA End-User Certificate portal**
  - "Remotely Piloted Aircraft" is a controlled category. Applications need a formal letter from the NCAA, company documents and a tax clearance certificate.
  - Private-company requests are recommended through the NSCDC Commandant General's office, all applications are screened by the DSS, and the portal says applications are free [S92][S93].
  - Telecom equipment needs NCC type approval as a supporting document [S95]. Check this for radio modules sold in the components store.
- **Indoor cage**
  - A snippet of the NCAA's Part 21 text lists "a model aircraft indoors" among exemptions (snippet) [S96]. The NCAA site was unreachable, so I could not confirm the scope.
  - Design the cage as a fully enclosed, netted indoor space, confirm the exemption with the NCAA's UAS Integration Unit, and require members' drones to be registered before any outdoor flight.

### 3.6 Biosafety (NBMA) for a shared BSL-1/BSL-2 lab

- **Legal base**
  - The NBMA Act 2015 makes NBMA the national biosafety authority, including approval of contained-use work (snippet) [S101].
  - The 2017 Regulations cover accreditation of institutions, certification of containment facilities (Part IV) and contained-use permits (Part V) (snippet) [S100].
- **Institutional Biosafety Committee**
  - Every institution doing modern-biotechnology research must set up an IBC, or use another institution's. It must also seek NBMA accreditation and containment certification.
  - The IBC has 5 internal members, including a Biosafety Officer as chair, plus 2 external community members, and reports annually to NBMA [S99].
  - Because the guidelines allow one institution to use another's IBC [S99], the lab could ask to use, for example, UNILAG's or NIMR's. I did not verify whether either has an accredited IBC.
- **Containment Level 2 facility rules (NBMA guideline, Mar 2017)**
  - An enclosed space with an anteroom.
  - Smooth, impermeable, cleanable benches and floors.
  - Biosafety cabinets tested every 12 months and after moves or filter changes.
  - Annual facility inspection, records kept for 5 years, and posted Containment Level 2 signs [S98].
- **2025 fees and timelines**
  - Institutional accreditation and facility certification: ₦500,000 processing (₦800,000 fast-track) plus a ₦100,000 permit.
  - New laboratory certification: ₦500,000 processing (₦600,000 fast-track) plus a ₦50,000 permit.
  - NBMA acknowledges an application within 90 days and decides within 170 days after acknowledgement [S97].
  - Plan for up to about 9 months (Estimate: 90 + 170 days = 260 days).
- **Not verified**
  - NBMA's documents above deal with GMOs. Pathogen work without genetic modification (true BSL-2 with human pathogens or clinical samples) likely falls to health regulators such as NCDC or MLSCN, but I could not confirm the rules.
  - Recommendation: open as a BSL-1 teaching and bio-maker lab (non-pathogenic organisms, GMO work only after NBMA certification), and add BSL-2 later with a partner IBC.

### 3.7 Chemical handling

- **ONSA End-User Certificate, "Chemicals" category**
  - Controlled chemicals are defined by an HS-code list.
  - Supporting documents include a NAFDAC permit, details of a qualified technical officer, storage and disposal details, and company and tax papers.
  - NAFDAC, the Ministry of Mines and the Ministry of Agriculture license chemical importers.
  - Chemicals used to make explosives fall under the same rules as explosives: magazine licence, customer list and vehicle details.
  - All applications are screened by the DSS [S94].
- **Implication:** keep a restricted-chemicals list, buy controlled reagents only through licensed importers, and do not stock explosive precursors.
- **Not verified:** Lagos State Safety Commission workplace registration and LASEPA/NESREA hazardous-waste requirements. The Commission's website was unreachable and searches had run out. Confirm these with a Lagos EHS consultant.
